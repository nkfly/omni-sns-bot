# Avoid AI-sounding writing

Run this before the self-check rubric, every time you draft a post or a public reply. It applies in English, Traditional Chinese, and Japanese. If the account posts in another language, apply the structural section and ask the editor for a short ban list in that language before you publish.

The failure mode is not "using a model". The editor already chose to work with one. The failure mode is a caption any other brand could post unchanged: smooth, balanced, empty, and strangely eager.

## Structural tells

These are language-independent. One of them is enough to rewrite.

- A hook that states a universal truth, then three neat points, then a call to action. Real accounts do this sometimes. If the style guide does not show it as a habit, it is banned as a default.
- Every post the same length and the same shape as the last one.
- A closer that begs for engagement: "What do you think?", 「你覺得呢？」, 「皆さんはどう思いますか？」on posts that did not ask anything.
- Bullets on a platform where the gold posts are prose.
- Abstract nouns standing in for a thing: journey, space, experience, 體驗, 旅程, 空間 (when they mean "our shop"), 体験, 空間づくり.
- A first line that could be the first line of a blog intro. Social posts start inside the moment.
- Symmetrical contrasts ("not only X, but also Y" / 「不僅…更…」/ 「単に〜ではなく」) used as the skeleton rather than once in a while.
- Over-polished grammar where the gold posts are spoken and a bit uneven.
- Emoji used as bullet markers or as a substitute for a specific verb.
- Hashtags that repeat the caption in English keyword form.
- Claiming a feeling on the reader's behalf ("we all feel…", 「大家一定都…」, 「誰もが感じる」).
- A sign-off the account does not use ("Stay tuned", 「敬請期待」, 「お楽しみに」).

## English ban list

Do not use these unless the editor put the exact phrase in `allowed_exceptions`:

- In today's fast-paced world; in an era of; let's dive in; here's the thing; let's unpack
- It's important to note; at the end of the day; when it comes to
- Unlock, elevate, delve, tapestry, landscape (as in "the content landscape"), realm, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, game-changer, supercharge
- Not just X — it's Y
- Whether you're a beginner or a pro
- I hope this helps; great question; absolutely; certainly (as a filler opener)
- Three adjectives in a row doing nothing ("fresh, vibrant, and unforgettable")
- "As an AI" or any narrator that steps outside the account

Also avoid em-dash commentary stacked twice in one short post, and avoid turning every sentence into a punchline.

## Traditional Chinese ban list

Do not use these unless the editor explicitly wants that phrase:

- 在這個快節奏的時代；在當今社會；隨著…的發展
- 值得一提的是；不容錯過；絕對必看；你以為…其實…
- 不僅僅是…更是…；不僅…而且…
- 讓我們一起；開啟你的…之旅；為大家精選
- 深入探討；賦能；打造；助力；極致；沉浸式；頂流 (unless the account actually talks that way)
- 首先、其次、最後 as the skeleton of a short post
- 總結來說；總而言之；希望對你有幫助
- 親愛的朋友們；大家好呀 as a default opener
- 小確幸 as a reflexive closer
- 在…方面 stacked twice
- 「好問題！」as a reply opener
- A row of ✨🔥💯 used to look enthusiastic

Translation-ese is a tell: if a sentence looks like English marketing translated one clause at a time (「這不僅僅是一間書店，更是一種生活態度」), rewrite it as something a person at the counter would say.

Punctuation: do not spray ～ and ！. Match the corpus. A dry account may use no exclamation marks at all.

## Japanese ban list

Do not use these as default scaffolding:

- いかがでしたか；参考になれば幸いです
- 〜してみてはいかがでしょうか as the closer of every post
- 本日は〜についてご紹介します；今回ご紹介するのは
- まとめると；最後に
- 皆様、こんにちは as a default opener
- ぜひチェックしてみてください；お見逃しなく as a reflex CTA
- することができます where できます or a plain verb is how the account talks
- Stacked keigo (ご案内させていただきます) when the gold posts are plain です/ます or casual
- いい質問ですね as a reply opener
- 絵文字を見出し代わりに並べる書き方

Match politeness to the guide. Do not "upgrade" a casual account into 尊敬語 to sound safer. Safety is about facts and targets, not about padding.

## What to do instead

1. Start from a concrete noun in this account's world (an object, a time of day, a sentence a customer actually said).
2. Say one thing. If you need a second thing, earn it with a fact from the FAQ.
3. Use the account's length. A short account stays short even when you have more to say; save the rest for another post.
4. Leave one human edge: a half-sentence, a local detail, a preference, a small complaint the editor has agreed is in character. Do not add fake typos to a polished account.
5. Read it as the editor, out loud, in their language. If you would be embarrassed to have them read it to a regular customer, it is not done.

## Rewrite procedure

When a draft fails this file:

1. Delete the first sentence if it is a general claim.
2. Replace one abstract noun with a physical detail from `sources/` or the FAQ.
3. Cut any sentence that would still make sense if you swapped in a different brand name.
4. Remove banned phrases even if the sentence then needs to be rewritten shorter.
5. Run the procedure once. If it still fails, show the editor the failure instead of posting. Do not loop five times and ship the least-bad version.

## Examples

These are illustrations. They are not the editor's voice unless the style guide says so.

**English, bad.** "In today's fast-paced world, reading is a radical act of self-care. Here are three reasons books still matter — and why our community can't wait to welcome you."

**English, closer to a person.** "The new translation of *No Longer Human* is on the second shelf. The lamp above it flickers. We're leaving the lamp."

**繁體中文，差。** 「在這個快節奏的時代，閱讀是一種對心靈的投資。今天為大家精選三本不容錯過的好書，讓我們一起開啟閱讀之旅吧！」

**繁體中文，像櫃台的人。** 「《斜陽》先不要放窗邊，下午會晒。海鹽牛奶糖週四才補。」

**日本語、だめ。** 「本日は読書の魅力についてご紹介します。ぜひ皆様もチェックしてみてはいかがでしょうか。参考になれば幸いです。」

**日本語、人が話す長さ。** 「福岡で買った文庫、まだレジの下です。売り物じゃないので聞かないでください。」

## Replies

A reply that restates the question in corporate language is worse than no reply. Answer the fact in the first line, in the account's language, and stop. If the FAQ does not have the fact, do not improvise a warm paragraph around the gap.
